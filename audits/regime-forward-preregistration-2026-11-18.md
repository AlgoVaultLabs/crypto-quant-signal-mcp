## 0. What is tested, in one paragraph

T3 rebuilt the regime label offline, on one venue's archives, and scored it. This study asks whether the labels production SERVED behave the same way on bars after T3's registration. The labels are the `regime_snapshots` rows the sampler writes at every closed bar (rule version 2; BINANCE, BYBIT, OKX and BITGET × 1h, 4h, 1d). For each venue it reads T3's answer 4 (agreement with key A, T3 §4.8 A4, with lag, flip-flops and confidence monotonicity as descriptives) and T3's answer 5 (persistence and swing size, T3 §4.8 A5), on closes from W0 = 2026-11-16T08:00:00Z to W1 = 2026-12-16T08:00:00Z, against T3's baselines for each answer. Each venue × answer gets one of three states: `PASS`, `FAIL` or `INDETERMINATE` (§6). There is one read, on or after 2026-12-30T08:00:00Z, behind a seal. Answers 1–3 are not scored.

Each timeframe has a look-ahead of 14 of its bars after W1, read in three ways only: to end a run that crosses W1 (A5, §4.3), and the closes from W1 to that end stay in A5's sequence, so baseline (c)'s block shuffle and a run's reference `c` read them as well; to date key A; and to end the lag of a key-A turn of the window, which may end on a look-ahead close before the next turn (§4.4). No close of the look-ahead is a row of A4, a run start, a flip-flop or a lag's turn. Key A also reads 14 bars of price history before W0. Absolute UTC throughout (arc lesson 8).

## 4. Methodology — T3's, carried by section

What is T5's own is the input: the served label series instead of a replay, on each venue's grid, with bar closes as rows. The statistics are T3's code, called unmodified from `src/scripts/regime-diag/**` (§10).

- **4.1 The served series.** For each kept coin × timeframe, close j of the venue grid is origin + j·I. Its label, confidence and volatility ratio are the snapshot row's; its close price is the close of the venue bar j − 1, the bar that close ends. T3 §4.1 (the replay) does not apply: the stored label is the label a caller received at that close. The volatility ratio is the stored value, rounded as production stores it (the other side of T3 §8, correction 15).
- **4.2 A4 — agreement with key A** (T3 §4.8 A4). Rows: every labelled close in [W0, W1) of every kept coin × timeframe that is TRENDING and whose bar j − 1 has a dated key-A phase. T3 §4.2's entries do not apply: a close is a row. Key A is T3 §4.9, per coin × timeframe on the venue's bars over key A's span. `g`, `A`, `A_0` and `S4 = A − A_0` are T3's, with ω of §3. Comparators: (c) the block shuffle of T3 §4.7, on the runs of equal label of each coin × timeframe sequence of the window's rows (a hole ends a block), phases and weights left in place; (d) the always-up, always-down and random labels, as T3 §4.8 states them. (a) and (b) do not apply.
- **4.3 A5 — persistence and swing size** (T3 §4.8 A5). Runs start at a close in [W0, W1). A run in progress at W0 is cut by the window edge and censored. A run that crosses W1 ends at its first label change within the 14-bar look-ahead: that close is given a non-trending label different from the one before it, so it ends the run and opens none, and nothing after it is read. A run with no change by the end of the look-ahead is censored. A run that starts at or after W1 is therefore never counted. A coin × timeframe's A5 sequence is its labelled closes from W0 to that end: baseline (c)'s block shuffle permutes the blocks of the whole sequence, the closes after W1 included, and a run's reference `c` is read where it lands. `y`, `c` and `R5` are T3's, with σ_bar from the stored volatility ratio.
- **4.4 Descriptives, no verdict** (T3 §4.8). Lag: key-A turns whose bar closes in [W0, W1), each measured to the first close whose label points the new way, which may be a close of the look-ahead, and censored at the next turn as T3 states it, per timeframe. Flip-flops: closes in [W0, W1) only, `N_ff = 3`. Confidence monotonicity: T3's `perm_test` (B = 1,000) of `g` over the five confidence bins, on A4's rows, run only when they meet the floor (§5). Run lengths per timeframe.
- **4.5 Clusters.** Coin × venue day: the 24 hours from the venue's daily boundary, which is T3's coin × UTC day on BINANCE and BYBIT and the same day shifted to 16:00Z on OKX and BITGET, so that a daily close is in its own day. For A5, the run's first close.
- **4.6 Resampling, level and seeds.** T3 §4.6 and §4.7: the cluster bootstrap within coin, `B = 10,000`; `B_c = 1,000` shuffles; `B = 1,000` permutations; one-sided level 0.025 through `ci_lower` and `ci_upper`; Kish-effective clusters. ## 5. Floors, power and the read date (PROCEDURE §5)

- **Unit and floor.** The unit is the coin × venue-day cluster (§4.5). The floor is 50 Kish-effective clusters (`FLOOR_CLUSTERS`), counted under ω on the rows a statistic uses, per venue. A statistic below it is too little data, by name. There is no row floor.
- **What is on disk before the seal** (arc lesson 4), per venue, in `label-floors.json`: the TRENDING closes of the window, an upper bound on A4's rows, which are the subset with a dated phase, and their Kish-effective clusters; A5's runs (§4.3), by T3's own `runsOf` on the labels with every bar taken as present, an upper bound on the runs read, since a run whose bars are missing is censored at the read, and their effective clusters by T3's `a5Eff`; the label-direction minority share of the TRENDING closes and its §4c bound; the number of day clusters; the drops; and the label-parity count. These need labels only. The counts are bounds; the effective clusters are a label-side estimate, not a bound, because Kish's count is not monotone in the rows, so the binding comparison with the floor is the one made at the read. Also before the seal: every bar the read scores, in the home's store, and `bars-manifest.json` with each stored answer's sha256 (§1).
- **What waits for the bars.** Key A is the outcome of A4: its phases come from bars after each close. So the dated share, the phase side of A4's identifiability and the effective clusters of A4's dated rows cannot exist before the seal. They are computed after it. Before `S4` or `R5` is computed, the effective clusters of the rows it reads — A4's dated rows, A5's runs — are compared with the floor: below it the answer is `INDETERMINATE`, its reading is null, and no bootstrap, shuffle or permutation runs for it. A §4c bound below the floor likewise stops `S4`. The confidence-monotonicity permutation reads A4's rows and waits on A4's comparison. The effective clusters and the shares are recorded in the result.
- **Power.** At CH1 (E4) every venue held, over 30 days, A4 rows and A5 runs whose clusters exceed the floor, and T3's instrument (T3 §5: `2.80·SD/√K`, SD 0.5, a cluster carrying one row) puts the detectable effect at coin-day clusters below the 3.0 pp floor on every venue. The figures are in the vault.
- **The `@day` reading is not identifiable by construction.** T3's `@day` sensitivity variant clusters by day alone. A 30-day window has at most 31 day clusters, below the 50-cluster floor, so the variant is `UNDEFINED` on every venue and both answers. It is reported as `UNDEFINED`, with its day count, and never as `FAIL` or as a state.
- **1d alone is floor-limited.** A daily series holds about 30 closes a coin in the window, and key A needs 7 bars on each side of a turn, so the dated daily rows are few. A4 is read pooled over the three timeframes under the census weights, as T3 §4.8 reads it, and no per-timeframe A4 state is read.
- **The underpowered branch.** An effect at the floor may return too little data. That is `INDETERMINATE`, reported with the interval's half-width and the effective clusters, as T3 §5 states. The window cannot be extended; a later window is a new registration.

## Identifiability (PROCEDURE §4c)

A4's floor is on a directional comparison: `S4` against chance and against the constant labels. `S4 = A − A_0` cannot exceed twice the smaller of the label's and the phase's minority-direction shares, so the bound is computed on that smaller share. The phase share needs bars and is not known before the read. The label share was measured at CH1 on rule-2 rows (E3), class `cardinality`, and is stated as a rounded lower bound.

| comparison | arm | minority-side share m (cardinality, with probe) | declared floor (pp) | bound 2m (pp) | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A4 primary `S4` against chance, the always-up, the always-down and the random label | each venue, TRENDING closes of the window: the smaller of the label's and the phase's minority shares | 0.15, a lower bound: the smallest TRENDING_UP or TRENDING_DOWN minority share over the twelve venue × timeframe cells of rule-2 rows at CH1 was at least this; probe: CH1's count of the two labels per venue × timeframe in `regime_snapshots` (E3); the phase share is recomputed at the read | 3.0 | 30.0 | IDENTIFIABLE |

At the read, before `S4` is computed, the harness recomputes both shares under ω on A4's realized rows, takes the smaller, and computes its bound through `attainable_bound_from_share` (`ops/monitoring/population_comparison.py`). ## 6. Decision rule (PROCEDURE §6)

Three states per venue × answer, T3 §6's fit-window states under this study's names: `PASS` is T3's `works`, `FAIL` is T3's `broken`, `INDETERMINATE` is T3's `too little data`, with its cause named (fewer than 50 effective clusters, `NOT_IDENTIFIABLE`, `BARS_UNAVAILABLE`, or an interval that reaches the floor while a criterion for `PASS` is unmet).

- **A4** — `PASS`: `point(S4) ≥ 3.0` pp; `LB > 0` for the excess over the always-up, the always-down and the random label; and the shuffle gives `p ≤ 0.025` with the interval of `S4` excluding the shuffle mean. `FAIL`: `UB(S4) < 3.0` pp, or `UB < 0` for one of those excesses, or `UB(S4)` below the shuffle mean.
- **A5** — `PASS`: `R5 ≥ 1.10`, `LB(R5) > 1` and shuffle `p ≤ 0.025`. `FAIL`: `UB(R5) < 1.10`.
- A point below the floor whose interval reaches it is `INDETERMINATE`, not `FAIL`. Work and failure at once is `INDETERMINATE` (T3 §6).
- **The lines.** The Mac read prints `REGIME_FORWARD_RUN_VERDICT=PASS | REFUSED — <reason> | INDETERMINATE — <reason>` (exit 0, 1, 3) and, on PASS, `REGIME_FORWARD_VERDICT=BINANCE:A4=<state>,A5=<state>;BYBIT:…;OKX:…;BITGET:…`. Callers gate on the tokens. The host read prints `OWED_READ_VERDICT=PASS | FAIL | INDETERMINATE`; it is `FAIL` only on a rule-2 `regime_since_ms` break, and `PASS` means the window's rows were written whole.
- **What follows.** A venue's answer that reads `PASS` is eligible for T7, and goes to Mr.1 before anything is public. `FAIL` and `INDETERMINATE` are recorded and go nowhere. The states and every figure are in the result, in the vault.

## 7. The post-result respecification test (PROCEDURE §7)

After the read, any proposed change to a statistic, a row set, a filter, a comparator, a weight, a cluster definition or a constant of this file is put to one question: **would this have been proposed had the check passed?** If not, it is a correction, registered in a new file and applied only to closes after this window's look-ahead, which nobody has seen. The window is spent by one read; the seal refuses a second, and no respecification reopens it. A defect found before the read, by the pre-read audit or by a test, is fixed and recorded as an appended amendment before the read.
